## Why it matters
Sleeping too little reduces attention, reaction speed and stress control. In space this is riskier because many tasks are critical for mission safety.

## What happens in space
Astronauts on the Space Station sleep on average about 6 hours a night, even though 8.5 are scheduled. Noise, artificial light, irregular schedules, stress and the lack of a natural day-night cycle all play a part. A study of 24 astronauts found that sleeping 6 hours or less is associated with slower reactions and more stress.

## Warning signs
Several nights in a row with less than 6 hours of sleep, daytime sleepiness, mistakes in usual tasks, irritability, low mood or slower reactions on the reaction test.

## What to do
- Log your hours of sleep every day.
- Keep a fixed sleep schedule when the mission allows it.
- Reduce screen light before sleeping and use the eye mask and earplugs from the sleeping bag.
- Avoid critical tasks when the reaction test shows a clear decline compared with your normal.
- If insufficient rest persists or affects your tasks, tell the crew member with the most medical training.
- When the signal is back, include it in the report for the doctors on Earth.

## When to alert
There is no validated number of nights to trigger an alarm in this prototype. Report persistent insufficient rest; if sleepiness affects a critical task, ask for support without waiting to reach a cutoff of days.
