## Why it matters
Space radiation is invisible and is one of the most important hazards of long journeys: it increases the long-term risk of cancer and can affect the nervous system.

## What happens in space
Outside Earth's protection, the crew receives more radiation. In 6 months on the Space Station a person receives roughly 80 to 160 mSv, much more than the 2 to 3.6 mSv a person receives per year on Earth. Solar storms can increase the dose within a few hours.

## How to measure on board
Each crew member wears a dosimeter. Check the accumulated dose and the mission's space weather alerts.

## Warning signs
A solar storm or particle event alert, an accumulated dose approaching the mission limit, nausea or feeling unwell after a solar event.

## What to do
- With a solar storm alert, go to the ship's shielded area according to the protocol.
- Check and log the dosimeter dose.
- When the signal is back, include the dose in the report for Earth.

## When to alert
Yellow: accumulated dose higher than planned. Red: a solar storm alert in progress or symptoms after a solar event.
