## Why it matters
Glucose is the sugar the body uses for energy. Very low values cause shaking, sweating, confusion or fainting; sustained high values can indicate a metabolic problem.

## What happens in space
The mission diet, stress, lack of sleep and less movement can change glucose. In the Inspiration4 crew tests published by NASA, each person's glucose changed between the samples before and after the flight: that is why it is best to compare with your own normal.

## How to measure on board
Measure with the kit's glucose meter, noting whether it was fasting or after eating, so you always compare in the same situation.

## Warning signs
A very low value with shaking, sweating, confusion or dizziness; a very high value that repeats; a value very different from your normal with no explanation (it may be a measurement error).

## What to do
- If the value looks strange, repeat the measurement before acting.
- If it is low and you have symptoms, follow the onboard protocol and alert the crew.
- Review meals, sleep and stress over the last few days.
- If it is repeatedly outside your normal, include it in the report for the doctors on Earth.

## When to alert
Yellow: an out-of-range value that repeats on the second measurement. Red: a very low value with symptoms or confusion.
