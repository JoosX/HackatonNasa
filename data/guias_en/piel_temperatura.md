## Why it matters
Temperature helps detect infections. The skin shows irritation, allergies or infections, which are common in a closed environment.

## What happens in space
Skin rashes were the most common medical event on the Space Station: about 1.1 per flight year. The immune system changes during flight and some dormant viruses can reactivate.

## How to measure on board
Measure temperature with the kit's thermometer, always the same way. For the skin, check the area in good light and note size, color and whether it itches or hurts.

## Warning signs
Fever, chills, a rash that grows or spreads, blisters, intense pain or fever together with other symptoms.

## What to do
- Log your temperature and repeat it if it is high.
- Keep the skin clean and dry; avoid scratching.
- Compare the affected area with previous days.
- If there is fever or the rash grows, alert the crew medical officer.

## When to alert
Yellow: slightly high temperature or a rash that does not improve. Red: high fever, a rash that spreads quickly or fever with other symptoms.
