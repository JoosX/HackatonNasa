## Why it matters
Blood oxygen shows whether the lungs are delivering enough oxygen to the body. On a ship, the air is recycled: if carbon dioxide (CO₂) rises or oxygen drops, headache, tiredness and poor concentration appear.

## What happens in space
The ship is a closed environment: the air depends on life support systems. NASA studies the risk of hypoxia (too little oxygen in the tissues), especially during spacewalks and when cabin pressure changes.

## How to measure on board
Place the pulse oximeter on a clean, warm finger, stay still and wait for the number to stabilize before writing it down.

## Warning signs
Oxygen lower than your normal, shortness of breath, bluish lips or nails, dizziness, confusion or headache in several crew members at once (it may indicate a problem with the ship's air).

## What to do
- Repeat the measurement at rest.
- Check the ship's air indicators (CO₂ and oxygen) with the crew.
- If several crew members have symptoms at the same time, alert immediately: it may be a problem with the air system.
- With shortness of breath or confusion, alert the crew immediately and follow the onboard protocol.

## When to alert
Yellow: a value below your normal that repeats. Red: a very low value, shortness of breath or confusion.
