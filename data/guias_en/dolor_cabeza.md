## Why it matters
Headache is common in space, especially during the first days. It is almost always mild, but it can be a sign of lack of sleep, dehydration, air with too much carbon dioxide or, rarely, something more serious.

## What happens in space
The shift of fluids toward the head, the closed environment of the ship, noise, stress and irregular schedules can cause headaches. A study of Space Station astronauts found that most had headaches during the mission.

## Warning signs
A very strong or sudden headache, the worst you have ever had; headache with vision changes, confusion, weakness, fever or vomiting; headache that gets worse over several days.

## What to do
- Review your recent data: hours of sleep, blood pressure and hydration.
- Rest in a quiet place with low light.
- Hydrate according to the mission plan.
- Check with the crew whether the ship's air indicators are normal.
- If the headache does not improve or keeps coming back, alert the crew medical officer.
- With a very strong headache or with vision changes or confusion: alert the crew immediately.

## When to alert
Yellow: a headache that lasts more than a few hours or comes back for several days. Red: a sudden, very strong headache, or one with vision changes, confusion, weakness or fever.
