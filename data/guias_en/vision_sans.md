## Why it matters
Good vision is needed to read screens, instruments and procedures. On long missions, the eyes can change.

## What happens in space
The shift of fluids toward the head can change the structure of the eye and the brain. NASA calls this Spaceflight Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome (SANS) and monitors it periodically.

## How to measure on board
Take the mission's periodic vision test and note whether things look blurry far away or up close, or whether you notice changes compared with previous days.

## Warning signs
New blurry vision, changes in near or far vision, or blurry vision together with a strong headache.

## What to do
- Log the change and when it started.
- Take the mission vision test.
- Alert the crew medical officer if the change is new or getting worse.
- If blurry vision comes with a strong headache, alert the crew immediately.

## When to alert
Yellow: a slight change in vision. Red: sudden loss of vision or blurry vision with a strong headache.
