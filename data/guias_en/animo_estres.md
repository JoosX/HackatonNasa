## Why it matters
Mood and stress affect sleep, concentration, teamwork and mission safety.

## What happens in space
Isolation, confinement, distance from Earth, workload and irregular schedules can affect the crew's mental health. NASA considers these risks important on long missions.

## How to measure on board
Log mood and stress every day on a scale from 1 to 5, without overthinking it. What matters is the trend over several days, not a single day.

## Warning signs
Low mood for several days in a row, irritability, withdrawing from the rest of the crew, sleeping much worse, losing interest in usual tasks or thoughts of self-harm.

## What to do
- Keep routines: sleep schedule, exercise and meals.
- Talk with a crewmate you trust.
- Use the moments of communication with family and psychological support when there is a signal.
- If low mood lasts several days, alert the crew medical officer.
- If there are thoughts of self-harm, alert the crew immediately.

## When to alert
Yellow: mood 2 or less for 3 days or high stress that does not go down. Red: mood 1, complete isolation or thoughts of self-harm.
